# Voice

The post is the owner's, written from the session. If the project rules name a reference post, read it before writing and match it. If the owner has a personal blog, read a few of their posts too: that is how a person sounds. These are the defaults.

## Who is talking

- **First person, the owner.** "I", and "we" for the owner and the AI working together.
- **No "I asked my AI".** It's not a story and it doesn't compel anyone. Mention the AI only where it is the point of the post, and never let it take credit for the owner's decisions.
- **The technology is the character.** Give the problem a personality: the save that throws away what it doesn't recognise, the health check knocking on a locked door, the card that keeps changing its mind. The post is about what a reader can learn from the mistakes.
- **The owner decides.** "I wanted…", "To do X, I needed Y." Not "I asked for", "I wished", "please": those read like a prayer.
- **The owner's own words, sparingly.** A line from the session where it beats any retelling, cleaned up: typos fixed, phrased as what was needed. A post is not a chat transcript.

## How it sounds

- **Write for visitors.** Leave out the project's plumbing (how many sites, which service, the session play-by-play). Say what happened in general tech words, not code-level ones.
- **Concrete beats general.** The actual error text, the actual number, the before and after.
- **Uneven rhythm.** Short fragments. An aside to the reader. An honest small embarrassment. No tidy parallel lists of three.
- **Vary the tone** within a post and from post to post: a scene, a confession, a dry joke, a plain explanation, a lesson. Not the same intro, middle and table every time.
- **Dry humour, no hype.** No "game-changer", "supercharge", "seamless", "unlock".
- **Honest.** What broke, what took three tries, what the AI confidently got wrong.
- **No em-dashes:** start a new sentence or use a comma. **They/them** for anyone whose pronouns aren't known.

## Leave room for the owner

The human touch is the owner's to add. Draft a plain intro line and plain asides, and mark where theirs belong:

```markdown
<!-- your aside: how it felt to see it finally work -->
```

Don't invent their history, opinions or jokes, and don't plant misspellings to sound human. When the owner edits a draft, learn from it: the project's rules file is the place to write down their style.

## Shape of a section

A heading that says what happened ("The list leaked drafts", not "Discovery"), then the moment, then what it taught. One idea per section.

## The outro

Every post ends with "what happened, how it was solved, how else it could be done". A reader on another stack should leave knowing which approach suits them. Usually a short paragraph or a list: each alternative, what it's better at, what it costs. A table only when several options compare on the same points:

| Approach | Better at | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| What we did | … | … |
| Another way | … | … |

Then one closing line they can use.
