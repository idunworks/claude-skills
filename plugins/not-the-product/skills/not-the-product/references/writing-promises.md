# Writing promises members can trust

The text is where a member decides whether to trust the place. Weigh every word.

## The method

1. **Start from what is true in the code today.** Read it. A promise about something not built yet is labelled as coming, or left out.
2. **Benefits first, then the reservations.** A first draft that opened with AI crawlers and bots that ignore requests made its own author say "I would only start a closed group". Every word was true; the order was wrong.
3. **Put the reservations behind a "Read more"** that opens in place. Not hidden text, not a separate legal page.
4. **Cut every word that promises more than you control.** You control your own site, not other people's copies, not bots that ignore you, not a search giant's summaries.
5. **Write a "why these words" note** for each choice, and keep it next to the text.
6. **Read it as a member would.** A member reads "a change" as something *they* do. Say whose change.
7. **The owner approves the final words.** Translations are made from the approved text and approved too.

## Words that keep promises honest

| Instead of | Write | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "They link back here" | "They usually link back here" | Some AI answers don't link |
| "Deleted content disappears" | "...disappears from here within 90 days" | You can't recall others' copies; give the real time |
| "We block AI training" | "We say no to AI companies training their AI on what you write" + read more: "Our no is a request that serious companies respect" | robots rules are requests |
| "We don't track you" | "We use no cookies, and nothing is saved that recognises you from one day to the next" | Concrete and checkable |
| "Your posts are deleted when you leave" | "Your posts no longer carry your name" | Anonymising keeps others' conversations whole; say what really happens |
| "We never email you" | "We never send you messages just to get you to come back" | Forbids the purpose, not useful mail the member asked for |
| "A change never applies to what you wrote" | "If we ever change these promises, we tell you before it happens. What you wrote before keeps the promises it was written under." | The first version was read as being about the member deleting posts |
| "Every decision is public" | "Every decision is recorded" | Say who can see the log |

## Example: public and closed groups

> **Public or closed?**
>
> A **public** group is open to everyone. What you write can help someone who has the same question, and it's how new people find the group.
>
> A **closed** group is only for its members.
>
> Whoever creates the group chooses which kind it is. You can see it before you join.
>
> **What you write in a public group**
>
> Search engines and AI assistants can read it when someone asks a question. They usually link back here.
>
> We say no to AI companies training their AI on what you write. That applies to all of them, equally.
>
> You can mark a post "only for members". Then only the group sees it.
>
> What you delete disappears from here within 90 days.
>
> *Read more:* Our no is a request that serious companies respect. A bot that ignores it can read a public page, just as a person can copy it. Some search engines read pages to write AI summaries, and today you can't always say no to that without leaving the search engine.

## Example: the member promises

> **No ads, nothing for sale**
> There are no ads here. We never sell or hand over anything about you, to anyone.
>
> **The newest comes first**
> Posts are shown newest first. Moderators can pin a few at the top. Nothing is pushed up because it gets more reactions or makes people angry.
>
> **We only notify you when it's about you**
> On the site you see when someone answers you, mentions you, or a decision concerns you. We never send you messages just to get you to come back.
>
> If you want, you can ask for a weekly summary by email. You turn it on yourself, and you can stop it with one click.
>
> **News and offers, only if you want them**
> If you want, you can get news and offers from us by email. You turn it on yourself, and you can stop it with one click. Your address is never sold or given to anyone else.
>
> Joining the community never puts you on a mailing list. What you write here is never used to choose what we offer you.
>
> **You can leave with your things**
> You can download everything you've written. If you delete your account, your posts no longer carry your name, and what you've deleted disappears from here within 90 days.

## Why equal treatment of AI companies

Refusing only the smaller AI companies while one large search company reads everything hands that company a monopoly on what your members know. The fair line is the same answer for all: everyone may read and link, nobody trains. If a member community ever chooses to allow training, it is allowed for all of them, and each member is told when they join.
