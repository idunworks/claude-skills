# Genres: slop and better, side by side

Each pair shows the same job done two ways, then what changed. The businesses and facts are invented to show the move. In real work, the "better" version is built from the author's real material. If a fact is missing, ask for it or leave a marked gap like `[number]`. Never fill it with something plausible.

## Landing page hero

> **Slop:** **Revolutionize your workflow.** The all-in-one platform that empowers teams to collaborate seamlessly and achieve more. *[Get started]*

> **Better:** **Timesheets that fill themselves from your calendar.** Approve a week in one click, send it to payroll as a file they already accept. *[Try it with last week's calendar]*

**What changed:** the headline names the product's actual job. The second line names the two steps a buyer cares about. The button says what happens when you press it.

## Error message

> **Slop:** Oops! Something went wrong. Please try again later.

> **Better:** We couldn't save your post: the connection dropped. Your text is still here. Press Save again, or copy it somewhere safe first.

**What changed:** what failed, what's safe, and what to do now. No cheerful "oops" when the user might have just lost work.

## Release note

> **Slop:** We're excited to announce a host of improvements and bug fixes designed to enhance your experience and make the app more powerful than ever.

> **Better:** Search now finds posts by their tags. Uploads larger than 20 MB no longer fail silently: you get a message with the limit. Fixed: the dark theme showed white text on white in the settings menu.

**What changed:** each line is a change a user can notice. "Bug fixes" turns into the bugs.

## Product page

> **Slop:** Meet the backpack that does it all. Designed for the modern explorer, it blends style and function in one sleek package. Whether you're commuting or climbing, we've got you covered.

> **Better:** 28 litres, 1.1 kg. The back panel opens like a suitcase, so you can reach the bottom without unpacking. Waxed cotton outside, so light rain runs off. The hip belt fits waists from 70 to 120 cm. It isn't fully waterproof: in a downpour, use a cover.

**What changed:** measurements, how the design solves a real annoyance, who it fits, and one honest limit. The limit is what makes the rest believable.

## Support reply

> **Slop:** Hi there! I completely understand your concern and I'm here to help. I've escalated this to the relevant team, who will look into it as a matter of priority. Is there anything else I can help you with today?

> **Better:** Sorry about that. Your order was charged twice because the payment page timed out and you pressed Pay again. I've refunded the second charge (order 4471). Your bank should show it within three working days. If it doesn't, reply here and I'll chase it.

**What changed:** it says what happened, what was done, when the customer will see it, and what to do if not. "Escalated to the relevant team" tells the customer nothing they can plan around.

## Blog post intro

> **Slop:** Have you ever wondered why some websites load instantly while others leave you waiting? Speed matters. In this article, we'll break down everything you need to know about performance, from images to caching.

> **Better:** Our shop's product pages took six seconds to load on a phone. Most of that was one image carousel nobody clicked. This is how we found it, and what replaced it.

**What changed:** it starts with the real problem and a number, names the cause, and promises one story instead of "everything you need to know".

## Social post with a lesson

> **Slop:** Unpopular opinion: most meetings should be emails. 🧵 After ten years in tech, here's what nobody tells you about productivity. (A thread.) Repost if you agree ♻️

> **Better:** We dropped our Monday status meeting in September and replaced it with a shared page everyone fills in by ten. Within a month two people had stopped reading it. Now the page ends with one question for each person, and they answer it in writing.

**What changed:** one real change, what went wrong with it, and what was done about that. No "unpopular opinion" that most readers already hold, no thread promise, no request to repost.

## Cold outreach email

> **Slop:** Quick question: are you the right person to talk to about growth? I'll keep this short. We help companies like yours 10x their conversions with our proven framework. Would it make sense to connect?

> **Better:** Hi Maria, your booking page asks for the date three times: once on the calendar, once in the form and once at checkout. We build booking forms for climbing gyms, and fixing that usually lifts completed bookings. I could send you a one-page mockup of yours. Worth a look?

**What changed:** it proves the sender looked at this company (one concrete observation), says who they are in one line, and asks for something small and specific. "10x" and "proven framework" promise without showing.

## Job application letter

> **Slop:** With over eight years of experience in fast-moving environments, I bring a unique blend of creativity and analytical thinking. I thrive under pressure and am passionate about making an impact.

> **Better:** At my last job I took over a help centre with 400 articles, half of them out of date. In a year I cut it to 120, and tickets about the topics I rewrote dropped by about a third. Your posting says the docs are "a known gap". That's the work I like most.

**What changed:** one real achievement with a number, tied to something in the job posting. Every applicant thrives under pressure; only this one cut 280 articles.

## Customer review

> **Slop:** Absolutely love it!!! Works exactly as described, arrived quickly, and the whole family uses it now. Can't imagine life without it. 10/10, would recommend!

> **Better:** I've used it every morning for three months. It grinds enough for two cups in about 20 seconds, and it's loud enough to wake the flat. Cleaning the burrs takes a brush and five minutes. I'd buy it again, but not if you share a wall with a light sleeper.

**What changed:** how long, how often, what it does in numbers, the downside, and who it's not for. Only someone who used it could write it.

## Closing paragraph of an opinion piece

> **Slop:** Ultimately, the choice is yours. Whether you're just starting out or a seasoned pro, the key is to stay curious, keep experimenting and never stop growing.

> **Better:** I'm still not sure this works for teams under five people. We had two people on it, and the overhead nearly ate the gains. If you've tried it smaller, I'd like to hear how it went.

**What changed:** it ends on an honest limit and an open question instead of advice for everyone. It adds something new rather than restating the thesis.

## Using these pairs

- **Don't copy them.** They show a move, not a template. A "better" that's reused unchanged becomes the next slop.
- **The better version is usually shorter in words and longer in facts.** If a rewrite comes out longer and no more specific, it isn't better.
- **Gather the material before writing.** The pairs above work because each "better" version has something real to say. Without it, no wording helps.
